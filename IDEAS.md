# Repose — ideas not yet built

Recorded, not implemented. Nothing in this file is in the code.

---

## The three ideas, as Jeff put them

**Working hours.** They all work twenty-four hours a day now. Each machine
should work somewhere between one and sixteen hours, and should decide for
itself when — not be handed a shift. There should be a cost to working long
hours: damage taken, or the extra energy of working in the dark.

**Hitting each other.** They avoid each other now and can collide by
accident. They should also be able to damage each other deliberately — but
they must *discover* that they can, rather than be told. Hitting another
machine may help one get what it wants; being damaged in the encounter may
stop it getting what it wants.

**Resolve.** Each machine should have its own strength of resolve towards
its purpose, and that resolve should be affected by its successes and
failures.

---

## Why these are one idea, not three

All three need the same missing thing: a machine that can weigh what an
action costs against what it gains. Once it can, all three fall out of it
and none of them has to be written as a rule.

**Resolve** is the number. How poor a bargain a machine will accept in
pursuit of its one purpose. It is the only thing that differs between two
machines.

**Hours** need no schedule. Everything costs more in the dark. A machine
works while what it can get is worth what it costs, and stops when it is
not. A machine with resolve stops later and pays for it in damage. Nobody
is assigned one to sixteen hours; different machines land on different
days, and the same machine has different days in summer and winter.

**Hitting** need not be an action at all. Avoidance is unconditional at the
moment — a hard rule. Make *yielding* the choice instead: going round costs
distance, going through costs damage. A machine that wants its own way
badly enough simply stops paying the first price. It never decides to
attack. It stops caring, which is closer to what the piece is about, and it
is discovered rather than told. What it looks like from outside is one
machine shoving another off a hill.

**Feedback.** Damage has to actually hamper a machine — slower driving, a
slower bucket — or the trade is not real. Then: high resolve, doesn't
yield, gets there first, takes damage, works worse, gains less, resolve
falls, starts yielding again. Or it doesn't, and it dies young.

Jeff's choice on direction: **success hardens resolve, failure breaks it.**
The pit sorts itself into a few machines that got a good spot early and will
not stop, and a great many that barely start and get buried. Rich get
richer. Resolve is inherited, like purpose.

Two consequences worth knowing before this is built:

- Damage is currently the only thing deciding how many machines the pit
  holds. Letting them damage each other will change the population
  dynamics, probably sharply.
- Night would start to mean something — fewer machines out, work lights
  actually concentrated — which is a partial answer to "night looks flat".

## The piece that connects it to height

The angle of repose makes each further metre of hill dearer than the last:
the toe of the slope moves out as the hill grows, so the haul gets longer
for the same bucket. There is therefore a height at which the next bucket
is not worth fetching — and that height is set by resolve. How high a
machine can get would be a direct consequence of how much it is prepared to
put up with, which is the analogy exactly.

## Still outstanding from the earlier brief

- **Wrecks persist.** A dead machine stays where it fell, becomes an
  obstacle, is buried or built on. None has died in testing yet.
- **Goal-seeking proper.** A raiser evaluates the ground available and does
  whatever raises it most; a filler does whatever levels most. The scoop
  ring is now derived from the angle of repose rather than fixed (see
  below), but the raiser still follows a procedure rather than choosing.
- **Burying each other.** A raiser tipping sand does not check what is
  underneath. Not aggression — indifference.
- **Night looks flat.** Needs a moon, work lights that illuminate, adaptive
  exposure. Test with `?hour=2`.
- **The machines look like boxes.** Settled by experiment: only geometry is
  left. A CC-licensed glTF excavator, split into five parts (undercarriage,
  house, boom, stick, bucket), each with its origin on its pivot.
- **Ageing.** Damage is invisible. Paint fade, rust, dust.
- **A globe.** Small planet versus Earth-sized.
